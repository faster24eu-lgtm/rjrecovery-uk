Typical stuck-vehicle jobs

  • Wheels sunk in a grass verge or a muddy field after parking at an event
  • A van stuck on a building site after rain
  • A car beached on a high kerb or a bollard, with the wheels off the ground
  • Slid into a ditch on a lane at the edge of London, on the Essex or Herts side
  • Stuck on ice or snow on a steep residential hill

What the operator does

They find a safe anchor, rig the winch to a proper recovery point on your car (not the bumper), and pull it out slowly. Then they check the tyres, the underside and the steering. If anything is damaged or leaking, the car goes on the bed instead of being driven away.

Help us find you

Fields and sites rarely have a postcode that takes you to the right spot. Send your live location on WhatsApp, or a what3words address, and tell us which way in is best for a truck.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Will winching damage my car?

Not when it’s attached to a proper towing eye or recovery point and pulled slowly. Damage usually comes from pulling on bumpers or suspension parts, or from spinning the wheels beforehand.
